How STP_Spacearm works
— explained with simple maths

A guide to the teammate's code: what each part does, the equations behind it in plain words, and how it compares with the Neural IK project.

Source: repository akashrayganguly/STP_Spacearm (commit a15a269) and its deck Spacearm_deck_v2.pptx. Read only, nothing was re-run: all numbers are the teammate's own reported results; figures are taken from the repository's reports/figures/. Neural IK numbers are the ones re-measured for the Neural IK deliverables.

1The idea in one page

A small satellite floats in space with a 7-joint robot arm on its top deck. When the arm moves, the satellite turns slightly the other way (like a skater pushing off). The task has two levels:

Level 1 · PlanGiven the arm's current joint angles and a target point, produce a smooth joint-space path that never touches the satellite or the arm itself.
→
Level 2 · FlyMove the arm there for real, every 0.1 s, while a ball suddenly appears, sensors are slightly wrong and motors are weak — and the satellite keeps turning.

The code is built from a few small pieces that each solve one sub-problem:

2The world: a floating satellite

The robot is generated from one settings file (configs/default.yaml): a 1 m cube bus (150 kg), two solar panels, a payload box and a 7-link arm made of capsules (18 kg). Total 212 kg; the arm is 8.5 % of the mass. Joint axes alternate z-y-z-y-z-y-z (shoulder 3, elbow 1, wrist 3), like a KUKA iiwa.

PyBullet runs with zero gravity and no damping, so nothing slows motion down and momentum is never lost. Starting at rest, the total momentum of the whole system stays zero:

$$\underbrace{\sum_i m_i\, v_i}_{\text{linear momentum}} = 0 \qquad\qquad \underbrace{\sum_i \big(I_i\,\omega_i + m_i\, r_i \times v_i\big)}_{\text{angular momentum}} = 0$$

In words: if the arm swings one way, the satellite must turn the other way so that the totals stay zero. The centre of mass does not move at all. In their test the arm turns joint 2 by 57° and the satellite turns 5.9° the other way.

Why it matters
A plan computed in the satellite's own frame is perfect in that frame, but when it is played on the floating satellite the tip misses a target fixed in space by 17.2 cm (median, max 33.3 cm). This single number is why Level 2 exists.

Timing: physics runs at 240 Hz, decisions at 10 Hz, so one decision lasts 24 physics steps (0.1 s). Joints are velocity-controlled, at most 0.5 rad/s.

3Arm maths: forward kinematics and the Jacobian

Forward kinematics (FK) answers: given the joint angles, where is the tool tip? It walks along the arm, one joint at a time. Start at the pedestal with position \(t\) and orientation \(R\) (a 3×3 rotation matrix), then for each joint \(i\):

FK chain$$t \;\leftarrow\; t + R\,e_z\,\ell_i, \qquad R \;\leftarrow\; R \cdot \mathrm{Rot}(\text{axis}_i,\, q_i), \qquad x_{\text{tip}} = t_7 + R_7\, e_z\, \ell_{\text{tool}}$$

Here \(\ell_i\) are the link lengths (24, 20, 20, 20, 20, 14, 12 cm), \(\ell_{\text{tool}} = 15\) cm and \(e_z = (0,0,1)\): "move along the link, then turn by the joint angle". It is written in PyTorch, so it is differentiable: the networks can learn through it. It matches PyBullet to 0.00012 mm.

The Jacobian \(J\) (3×7) says how the tip moves for small joint motions:

$$\Delta x_{\text{tip}} \;\approx\; J(q)\,\Delta q \qquad\text{(3 numbers out, 7 numbers in)}$$

Because 7 joints control only 3 numbers (position), there are many ways to reach the same point. The "spare" freedom is called the null space; it is used later to lean the elbow away from the satellite without moving the hand.

4Making the data

No dataset exists for this robot, so it is generated. For each random pose \(q\), PyBullet measures exact, noise-free labels:

$$q \;\longmapsto\; \big(\,d_{\text{body}}(q),\; d_{\text{self}}(q),\; x_{\text{tip}}(q)\,\big), \qquad d<0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{overlapping (collision)}$$
  • 150,000 uniform poses (75 %): every joint uniform inside the central 98 % of its range.
  • 50,000 near-contact poses (25 %), made by "nudging": take a pose that is already within 5 cm of contact, add small random noise, keep it only if it is still within 5 cm:
    $$q' = q + \varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\;0.05^2)\ \text{rad per joint}, \qquad \text{keep if } \big|\min(d_{\text{body}}, d_{\text{self}})\big| < 5\ \text{cm}$$
  • Test set: 20,000 uniform poses with a different random seed, never used for training.

Effect: the share of training poses near a surface goes from 7.7 % to 30.9 %, so the network sees many examples exactly where decisions are made. Generation takes 38 s (0.19 ms per pose) and is byte-for-byte reproducible from the seed.

5DistanceNet: "how close am I to touching?"

PyBullet can measure the gap, but only as a number (0.16 ms per pose) — it does not say which way to move to be safer. A small smooth network learns the gap and, through its gradient, the direction.

Network

$$\underbrace{[\sin q,\ \cos q]}_{14\ \text{inputs}} \;\xrightarrow{\;256\;}\; \text{SiLU} \;\xrightarrow{\;256\;}\; \text{SiLU} \;\xrightarrow{\;256\;}\; \text{SiLU} \;\longrightarrow\; (\hat d_{\text{body}},\ \hat d_{\text{self}}), \qquad \text{SiLU}(z) = z\,\sigma(z)$$

Sine and cosine are used because angles wrap around (+180° and −180° are the same). SiLU is a smooth activation, so the gradient \(\nabla c\) is smooth too. 135,938 parameters (0.5 MB).

Clearance: a cautious soft minimum

soft-min, τ = 1 cm$$c(q) \;=\; -\tau\,\log\!\Big(e^{-\hat d_{\text{body}}/\tau} + e^{-\hat d_{\text{self}}/\tau}\Big), \qquad \min(\hat d_{\text{body}},\hat d_{\text{self}}) - \tau\ln 2 \;\le\; c \;\le\; \min(\hat d_{\text{body}},\hat d_{\text{self}})$$
Worked example
If \(\hat d_{\text{body}} = 2\) cm and \(\hat d_{\text{self}} = 10\) cm: \(c = -\ln(e^{-2}+e^{-10}) \approx 1.9997\) cm — almost exactly the smaller gap.
If both are 3 cm: \(c = 3 - \ln 2 \approx 2.31\) cm — slightly below the true gap, i.e. on the safe side. Unlike a hard min, its gradient does not jump when the closest surface changes.

Training loss: precise where it matters

β = 2 cm$$\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{mean}\big[\,w \cdot \ell_\beta(\hat d - d)\,\big], \qquad \ell_\beta(e) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{e^2}{2\beta} & |e| < \beta \\[4pt] |e| - \dfrac{\beta}{2} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}, \qquad w = \begin{cases} 10 & |d| < 5\ \text{cm} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

In words: small mistakes are squared (be precise), big mistakes count only linearly (a few deep collisions cannot dominate), and mistakes near a surface count 10×. Targets are clipped to −5…+30 cm: beyond that no decision changes. Training: AdamW, learning rate \(2\times10^{-3}\) with cosine decay, 60 epochs, batch 1,024, 49 s on 4 CPU threads.

6TrajNet: one forward pass proposes a whole path

Input: the start pose and the target point. Output: 35 numbers that describe a smooth path.

$$\underbrace{[\sin q_0,\ \cos q_0,\ x^\*]}_{17} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{MLP } (512\times 3,\ \text{SiLU}) \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{u}_{35 \,=\, 7\ (\text{goal}) \,+\, 4\times 7\ (\text{middle})}$$

Step 1 — squash into the joint limits

$$q = m + h\,\tanh(u), \qquad m = \frac{q_{\max}+q_{\min}}{2}, \quad h = \frac{q_{\max}-q_{\min}}{2}$$

Because \(\tanh\) is always between −1 and 1, every control pose lies inside the joint limits — whatever the network outputs.

Step 2 — a Bézier curve through 8 control poses

degree-7 Bézier$$q(s) = \sum_{k=0}^{7} \binom{7}{k}\, s^k (1-s)^{7-k}\, P_k, \qquad P_0 = P_1 = q_0,\quad P_6 = P_7 = q_{\text{goal}},\quad P_2..P_5 = \text{middle poses}$$
  • Each pose on the path is a weighted average of the 8 control poses (the weights add up to 1). So if all control poses are inside the limits, the whole path is too ("convex hull" property).
  • The speed at the ends is \(q'(0) = 7(P_1 - P_0) = 0\) and \(q'(1) = 7(P_7-P_6)=0\): the arm starts and stops at rest.

Step 3 — learning without example paths

A target point can be reached by infinitely many poses and paths; copying examples would average several right answers into a wrong one. So TrajNet is self-supervised: it lowers a score that checks the outcome.

score to minimise$$J(\theta) = \underbrace{100\,\big\|\mathrm{FK}(q_{\text{goal}}) - x^\*\big\|^2}_{\text{reach the target}} \;+\; \underbrace{1000\cdot\frac{1}{16}\sum_{k=1}^{16} \max\!\big(0,\ 0.05 - c(q(s_k))\big)^2}_{\text{stay 5 cm clear (DistanceNet)}} \;+\; \underbrace{0.1 \sum \|\Delta q\|^2}_{\text{smooth}}$$
Worked example (units: metres)
A 1 cm reach miss costs \(100 \times 0.01^2 = 0.01\). Being 3 cm too close on average costs \(1000 \times 0.03^2 = 0.9\) — ninety times more. So the network learns: first never come close, then reach precisely.

552,483 parameters; 10,000 training steps × 256 queries; 3 min 11 s on 4 CPU threads. Alone it succeeds on 85 % of random and 66 % of hard queries.

2 · Refine ×30Adam on the 35 numbers, ≈ 90 ms
→
3 · Polish ×10exact IK on the goal pose, ≈ 5 ms
→
4 · VerifyPyBullet, 100 poses, ≈ 15 ms

Refine: improve this one path (test-time optimisation)

The network is frozen; the 35 numbers \(\theta\) of this path are adjusted directly on the same score \(J\):

$$\theta \;\leftarrow\; \theta - \text{Adam}\big(\nabla_\theta J(\theta)\big), \qquad 30\ \text{steps (100 on a retry)}$$

Adam is gradient descent with a per-number step size that adapts to how noisy each gradient is. Refining buys clearance, but it can pull the tip 1–2 cm off the target.

Polish: put the tip exactly on the target

Only the goal pose moves, with damped least squares on the exact FK (10 iterations, \(\lambda = 0.01\)):

DLS IK step$$\Delta q = J^{\mathsf T}\big(J J^{\mathsf T} + \lambda^2 I\big)^{-1}\big(x^\* - x_{\text{tip}}\big)$$

In words: "move the joints the smallest amount that moves the tip toward the target"; the \(\lambda^2 I\) term keeps steps sane near awkward (singular) poses.

Verify

PyBullet checks 100 poses along the path: every clearance \(> 0\), tip within 2 cm, all joints inside limits. If anything fails, plan once more with 100 refine steps (needed on 0.5 % of random queries).

8Level 2: flying the arm in a closed loop

One episode ("mission"):

  • Start: random pose at least 5 cm from contact. Target: fixed in space, ≥ 20 cm away, within 85 % of reach.
  • Success: \(\|x_{\text{tip}} - x^\*\| < 2\) cm and tip speed \(< 5\) cm/s. Failure: any contact (body, self, ball) or 20 s.

The robot only sees 56 measured numbers: joint readings and speeds, the camera vector tip→target, gyro spin, ball position and size, DistanceNet clearance and direction, and the previous and reflex commands.

The reflex: a hand-written baseline (no learning)

reflex prior$$\dot q_{\text{reflex}} = \underbrace{J^{+} v}_{\text{aim}} \;+\; \underbrace{(I - J^{+}J)\; k_{\text{null}}\, w_s\, \nabla c}_{\text{lean off the satellite}} \;+\; \underbrace{k_{\text{obs}}\, w_o\, \nabla d_{\text{ball}}}_{\text{push off the ball}}$$
$$v = \operatorname{clip}_{\|\cdot\|\le 0.15\,\text{m/s}}\big(k_p\,(x^\* - x_{\text{tip}})\big), \qquad J^{+} = J^{\mathsf T}\big(JJ^{\mathsf T} + 0.05^2 I\big)^{-1}$$
  • Aim: move the tip straight toward the target (\(k_p = 1\) /s, at most 15 cm/s).
  • Lean off: \((I - J^{+}J)\) is the null-space projector — it keeps only joint motions that do not move the hand. The 7th joint swings elbow and wrist away from the satellite (\(k_{\text{null}} = 0.5\) rad/s). The weight \(w_s\) ramps from 0 to 1 as clearance falls from 10 cm to 0.
  • Push off: move the joints away from the ball (\(k_{\text{obs}} = 0.5\) rad/s); \(w_o\) ramps 0 → 1 as the ball gap falls from 15 cm to 0.
  • If any joint would exceed 0.5 rad/s, the whole vector is shrunk by the same factor.
Weak spot
With a ball right on the straight line, "pull to the target" and "push off the ball" cancel out (a local minimum): the arm hovers 8–12 cm away until the 20 s run out.

9The RL correction (residual PPO-Lagrangian)

Instead of learning everything from scratch (which gave 0 % success after 130k steps), a tiny network learns only a bounded correction on top of the reflex:

command sent to motors$$\dot q_{\text{cmd}} = \operatorname{clip}\Big(\dot q_{\text{reflex}} + 0.5\cdot \text{fade}\cdot a\Big), \qquad \text{fade} = \min\!\Big(1,\ \frac{\|x_{\text{tip}} - x^\*\|}{10\ \text{cm}}\Big), \qquad a = \pi_\phi(o) \in [-1,1]^7$$

The correction has at most half the authority and fades out in the last 10 cm, so the reflex alone makes the precise final approach.

The policy network

$$o \in \mathbb{R}^{56} \;\longrightarrow\; 64\ (\tanh) \;\longrightarrow\; 64\ (\tanh) \;\longrightarrow\; a \in \mathbb{R}^7, \qquad 56\cdot64+64 + 64\cdot64+64 + 64\cdot7+7 + 7 = 8{,}270\ \text{parameters}$$

(The last 7 are the exploration noise levels.) It runs in 0.012 ms with NumPy.

Reward and cost

reward per step$$r_t = 10\,(D_{t-1} - D_t) \;-\; 0.01\,\|a_t - a_{t-1}\|^2 \;-\; 0.005 \;+\; 5\cdot[\text{success}] \;-\; 0.02\,\|a_t\|^2$$

\(D_t\) is the tip-to-target distance, so the first term pays for progress; the others discourage jerky corrections, wasted time and large corrections. Safety is not in the reward — it is a separate cost:

$$c_t = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if anything is closer than 2 cm (a near miss)} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}, \qquad \text{budget: } \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_t c_t\Big] \le 1 \text{ per episode}$$

PPO: careful policy updates

clipped objective, ε = 0.2$$L(\phi) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\min\big(\rho\,\hat A,\ \operatorname{clip}(\rho,\,1-\varepsilon,\,1+\varepsilon)\,\hat A\big)\Big], \qquad \rho = \frac{\pi_\phi(a\mid o)}{\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid o)}$$

\(\hat A\) (the advantage) says whether an action turned out better than expected. The clip stops any single update from changing the policy too much. Advantages are estimated with GAE (\(\gamma = 0.99\), \(\lambda_{\text{GAE}} = 0.95\)):

$$\hat A_t = \sum_{l\ge0} (\gamma\lambda_{\text{GAE}})^l\,\delta_{t+l}, \qquad \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$$

The Lagrangian part: a self-adjusting safety price

dual ascent$$\hat A = \frac{\hat A_r - \lambda\,\hat A_c}{1+\lambda}, \qquad \lambda \;\leftarrow\; \operatorname{clip}\Big(\lambda + 0.05\,\big(\bar C - 1\big),\ 0,\ 10\Big)$$

\(\bar C\) is the average near-miss count per episode. If the policy goes over budget, the price \(\lambda\) rises and safety weighs more; if it stays under, \(\lambda\) falls. (In their runs \(\lambda\) never rose above 0.45, so most of the caution came from the reflex and the shield.)

Asymmetric critics: the teacher sees more than the student

Two value networks (\(V_r\) for reward, \(V_c\) for cost; 90 → 256 → 256 → 1, 89,345 parameters each) are used only during training. They see the 56 sensor numbers plus 34 hidden truths (true joint angles, encoder bias, motor gains, satellite orientation, true clearances, ball velocity, time left). The policy that flies sees only the 56 sensor numbers. Training: 3.0 M steps, 4 simulators in parallel, difficulty ramped from 0 to 1 over the first half, ≈ 1 h 45 min on 4 vCPU.

10The safety shield: one last check

Every command passes a model-based filter before it reaches the motors. It predicts the pose 0.2 s ahead (2 control steps) for three step sizes:

$$q'(s) = q + s\cdot \dot q_{\text{cmd}}\cdot 0.2\ \text{s}, \qquad s \in \{1,\ \tfrac12,\ \tfrac14\}$$
safety function$$h(q) = \min\Big(\,c(q) - 6\ \text{cm},\;\; g_{\text{ball}}(q) - 5\ \text{cm}\,\Big), \qquad \text{accept the largest } s \text{ with } h(q'(s)) \ge 0$$

If no step size is safe, the arm moves along \(\nabla h\) (escape), or stops. The ball gap is pure geometry: each arm link is a capsule (a line segment with radius \(r_{\text{link}}\)) and the ball a sphere (centre \(b\), radius \(r_b\)):

$$g_{\text{ball}} = \min_{\text{links}}\Big(\operatorname{dist}\big(b,\ \text{segment}\big) - r_{\text{link}} - r_b\Big)$$
Why 6 cm and not 5 cm?
At 5 cm, 2 of 100 episodes grazed the satellite: after a joint slip the measured angles carried up to 3.6° of bias, so DistanceNet (fed with wrong angles) was ≈ 5.5 cm too optimistic. At 6 cm: 0 collisions in all scenarios, for about 2 points of success.

Cost of the shield: 0.18 ms per accepted command (3.3 ms when it has to escape). It intervenes on 9–15 % of steps.

13Side by side with Neural IK

Both projects follow the same pattern — a fast neural guess, an exact classical finish, an independent PyBullet check, and a learned correction on top of a classical controller — but they solve different versions of the problem.

Neural IK in the same notation

network output → angle$$[\,x^\*,\ \text{quat}^\*,\ q_{\text{seed}}\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{13} \;\longrightarrow\; (\sin\hat q,\ \cos\hat q) \in \mathbb{R}^{12}, \qquad \hat q_i = \operatorname{atan2}(\sin\hat q_i,\ \cos\hat q_i)\ \ (\pm 360^\circ \text{ into the limits})$$
collision-aware loss$$\mathcal{L} = \operatorname{MSE}\big(\text{sin/cos}\big) + 0.05\cdot \operatorname{mean}\ \sigma\big(C(\hat q)\big)$$

\(C\) is a frozen yes/no self-collision classifier (6 → 64 → 64 → 1). Its probability is a soft penalty; it does not measure distance.

Levenberg–Marquardt refinement$$\Delta q = \big(J^{\mathsf T}J + \lambda I\big)^{-1} J^{\mathsf T} e, \qquad \lambda = \|e\|^2 + 10^{-6}, \qquad e = (\text{position error},\ \text{orientation error}) \in \mathbb{R}^6$$
Level 2 command$$\Delta q_{\text{cmd}} = \Delta q_{\text{potential field}} + 2\cdot \pi_\phi(o), \qquad o \in \mathbb{R}^{101},\ \ \pi_\phi: 2\times64\ \tanh,\ 11{,}078 \text{ parameters}$$

The Level 2 success rates are not directly comparable: the teammate's world is harder (floating base, noisy sensors, target fixed in space, 2 cm tip tolerance), while Neural IK's target tolerance is far stricter in Level 1 (1 mm and 1° on the full pose).
What each could borrow
From STP_Spacearm: a distance network with a gradient instead of a yes/no classifier; checking the whole path; a safety shield (removed all collisions there); a free-floating base and a 7th joint for the null-space lean-off; training critics on hidden simulator truths.
From Neural IK: full 6-D pose accuracy (orientation matters for grasping); tests from home and random starting poses; an "LM alone" ablation showing what the network really contributes.
